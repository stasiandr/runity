# Proprietary / in-house game engines: strengths, famous problems, developer testimony (as of Sept 2026)

Scope note: ~22 search/fetch calls. Coverage is strongest for Frostbite, REDengine, Slipspace, RE Engine, Decima, id Tech, Luminous. Weak/no primary evidence gathered for Source 2, Northlight, Fox Engine, Glacier, 4A, RAGE, Snowdrop/Anvil specifics (see Gaps). Several fetches of PC Gamer / Automaton / TweakTown returned truncated pages or 403; where only search-snippet summaries were available this is flagged.

## Q1. For each engine: what is praised, what are the known pains, what have developers said publicly?

### Takeaway
Developer testimony splits cleanly: engines built for one genre by one core team (id Tech for shooters, Frostbite for Battlefield, RE Engine for linear action/horror, Decima for Guerrilla/Kojima open worlds) earn praise for performance and control. Pain shows up when the engine is pushed onto a genre or organization it wasn't built for: Frostbite on BioWare RPGs, RE Engine on open worlds, Luminous shared between teams, Slipspace/REDengine across multiple parallel projects.

### Cited Findings

**Frostbite (EA / DICE), mostly HISTORICAL (2011–2019) plus Battlefield 6 (2025)**
- EA made Frostbite the standard engine across its studios. Former EA executive Patrick Söderlund pushed this to standardize technology and cut licensing costs. — [Kotaku, Schreier, "How BioWare's Anthem Went Wrong" (2019)](https://kotaku.com/how-biowares-anthem-went-wrong-1833731964)
- Verbatim developer quotes from Schreier's Anthem report (19 sources):
  - "Frostbite is full of razor blades."
  - "Frostbite is like an in-house engine with all the problems that entails—it's poorly documented, hacked together, and so on."
  - "If it takes you a week to make a little bug fix, it discourages people from fixing bugs."
  - "The biggest problem I had with Frostbite was how many steps you needed to do something basic."
  - "It's hard enough to make a game. It's really hard to make a game where you have to fight your own tool set all the time."
  - Source for all five: [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/how-biowares-anthem-went-wrong-1833731964)
- Frostbite was built for shooters, so BioWare had to build save/load systems and third-person cameras from scratch for Dragon Age: Inquisition, starting in 2011. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/how-biowares-anthem-went-wrong-1833731964); [Screen Rant summary](https://screenrant.com/anthem-frostbite-engine-development/)
- Anthem: Frostbite could render "giant, gorgeous environments" but couldn't support the survival features in early designs. The central Frostbite support team was understaffed and stretched across EA studios. — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/how-biowares-anthem-went-wrong-1833731964); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/79501-anthem-development-plagued-mismanagement-frostbite-challenges-report.html)
- Mass Effect: Andromeda (2017): per a developer, Frostbite "is wonderful for rendering and lots of things" but didn't "have an animation system" out of the box. BioWare Montreal built its animation rigs from scratch. A later animation system called ANT was full of "duct-taped issues." The Inquisition team had built only some of the RPG tools, so the Andromeda team had to build many of their own. — [Kotaku, Schreier (2017)](https://kotaku.com/the-story-behind-mass-effect-andromedas-troubled-five-1795886428); [GamesRadar summary](https://www.gamesradar.com/mass-effect-andromedas-animation-problems-attributed-to-frostbite-understaffed-departments-and-last-minute-changes/)
- A former BioWare GM also said Frostbite "can be hard to manage." — [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/ea-frostbite-engine-difficulties-bioware-anthem-mass-effect/) (secondary; original interview not verified)
- Dragon Age: The Veilguard (Oct 2024) still shipped on Frostbite. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Age:_The_Veilguard). I found no new primary developer testimony (2024–2026) specifically about Frostbite pain on Veilguard. Its creative director's interviews: [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/rs-gaming/dragon-age-the-veilguard-john-epler-interview-1235147001/), [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/dragon-age/dragon-age-the-veilguard-john-epler-interview/).
- Counterpoint, Battlefield 6 (Oct 2025), where Frostbite is on home turf: Digital Foundry called the PS5 version "an excellent release" with "rock-solid performance." It also ran well on Xbox Series S. — [ResetEra thread for the DF review](https://www.resetera.com/threads/digital-foundry-battlefield-6-playstation-5-df-review-rock-sold-performance-excellent-multiplayer.1321099/); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/battlefield-6-runs-shockingly-well-on-xbox-series-s)
- For BF6, developers re-engineered memory management to fit Series S, and the gains carried over to all platforms. BF6 skipped hardware ray tracing to protect performance. The technical director explained why Frostbite suits the game. — [TweakTown interview](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/107400/battlefield-6s-technical-director-tells-us-why-frostbite-is-the-perfect-engine-for-the-game/index.html) (403 on fetch; claims come from search summary); [TechPowerUp benchmark](https://www.techpowerup.com/review/battlefield-6-performance-benchmark/)

**REDengine (CD Projekt RED), abandoned for UE5**
- CDPR's VP of Technology Charles Tremblay, speaking to Eurogamer (Dec 2024): "the whole team, myself included, are extremely proud of the engine we built for Cyberpunk. So it is not about, 'This is so bad that we need to switch'."
- On the real driver: "if we wanted to have a multi-project at the same time, building in parallel, sharing technology together, it is not easy." And: "we can finally have all the technical people in the company working together on different projects, rather than super centralised into one technology." — [Gamepressure (quoting Eurogamer)](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/cd-projekt-explained-its-decision-to-switch-from-redengine-to-ue5/z67609); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/the-witcher/cd-projekts-switch-to-unreal-wasnt-motivated-by-cyberpunk-2077s-rough-launch-or-a-this-is-so-bad-we-need-to-switch-situation-says-senior-dev/)
- The Matrix Awakens UE5 demo was the trigger, not the Cyberpunk launch. CDPR's first answer to "is UE5 ready for our kind of game" was "no" at the entry level. They decided a partnership with Epic to co-develop the needed features could close the gap. — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/cd-projekt-explained-its-decision-to-switch-from-redengine-to-ue5/z67609); [MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/cd-projekt-explains-reason-it-switched-to-unreal-engine-5)
- The co-CEO repeated that the switch wasn't caused by Cyberpunk's launch and that the company's goal was never to "make technology." — [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/cd-projekt-reds-move-from-redengine-to-ue5-for-the-witcher-4-wasnt-because-of-cyberpunks-launch-co-ceo-says/); [FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/news/as-cyberpunk-2-continues-on-unreal-cd-projekt-ceo-says-redengine-was-not-really-the-issue-they-just-never-wanted-to-make-technology/)
- CDPR restated its reasoning again around Sept 18, 2026. — [Aroged (2026-09-18)](https://www.aroged.com/2026/09/18/the-witcher-4-cd-projekt-red-justifies-the-switch-to-unreal-engine-5/) (not fetched; content unverified)

**Halo Slipspace (343 / Halo Studios), abandoned for UE5 (announced Oct 2024)**
- Studio Art Director Chris Matthews: "Respectfully, some components of Slipspace are almost 25 years old." Also: "Nanite and Lumen offer us an opportunity to do that in a way that the industry hasn't seen before."
- COO Elizabeth van Wyck on hiring and onboarding: "How long does it take to ramp somebody up to be able to actually create assets that show up in your game?" And: "The way we made Halo games before doesn't necessarily work as well for the way we want to make games for the future."
- Studio head Pierre Hintze tied the switch to faster content delivery.
- Source for all of the above: [Xbox Wire (Oct 6, 2024)](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2024/10/06/halo-studios-unreal-engine-interview/)
- Secondary coverage adds: a big share of staff was tied up maintaining Slipspace instead of making content; the engine slowed Halo Infinite; UE5 lets the studio run multiple teams in parallel. — [Pure Xbox](https://www.purexbox.com/news/2024/10/halo-dev-explains-decision-behind-ditching-its-old-game-engine); [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/halo-unreal-engine-5-move-developer-explanation/)

**RE Engine (Capcom)**
- Praise: strong for linear games. Resident Evil entries are the standard example. — [Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/monster-hunter-wilds-was-moving-to-the-re-engine-a-good-or-bad-idea) (opinion piece); [Creative Bloq on how RE Engine enabled MH Wilds](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/video-game-design/how-monster-hunter-wilds-was-made-possible-by-capcoms-secretive-re-engine)
- Dragon's Dogma 2 (Mar 2024): Capcom's own statement said a large amount of CPU is spent on each character, including physics of their presence. It said that "in situations where numerous characters appear simultaneously" CPU use can be very high and hurt frame rate. Capcom added: "settings that reduce GPU load may currently have a limited effect; however, we are looking into ways to improve performance in the future." — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/dragons-dogma-2-npcs-are-making-cpus-weep-and-tanking-the-frame-rate-but-capcom-is-looking-into-ways-to-improve-performance-in-the-future/); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/dragons-dogma-2-pc-performance-issues/)
- Monster Hunter Wilds (Feb 2025): widely criticized PC performance. In an earnings call, Capcom said the know-how from dealing with "increasing program complexity and performance challenges" in Wilds would help future open-world titles. Fixes took roughly a year of patches. — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/monster-hunter-wilds-performance-challenges-will-benefit-future-games-capcom-says/). Player sentiment that "RE Engine can't do open worlds": [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2246340/discussions/0/4847652375938887458/) (community opinion only).

**Creation Engine / Creation Engine 2 (Bethesda)**
- Starfield (2023) runs on Creation Engine 2. Creation Engine 3 is reportedly in development for The Elder Scrolls VI and Fallout 5. — [Wikipedia: Creation Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_Engine); [Wikipedia: Starfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfield_(video_game))
- The main player criticism is frequent loading screens and no seamless flight or landing, despite marketing Starfield as a space game. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1716740/discussions/0/4035851135388124215) (community sentiment, not dev testimony)
- Oblivion Remastered (Apr 2025, Virtuos with Bethesda) keeps the original Gamebryo-based engine for game logic, physics and scripts. Unreal Engine 5 only renders graphics and UI. Nearly 400 Virtuos developers worked on it at peak. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elder_Scrolls_IV:_Oblivion_Remastered); [UESP](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Oblivion:Oblivion_Remastered); [Tildee on Virtuos](https://www.tildee.com/behind-the-scenes-how-400-developers-from-virtuos-revamped-the-elder-scrolls-iv-oblivion-remastered/)

**Decima (Guerrilla / Kojima Productions)**
- Kojima (2017): "Their engine is well suited to the tools and systems of an open-world game. This was a very important aspect for us." Guerrilla and Kojima Productions share one codebase and pass code back and forth. KojiPro also placed a satellite team at Guerrilla. — [PlayStation Blog (2017)](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/02/23/the-hideo-kojima-death-stranding-interview-strands-decima-and-guerrilla-games/); [Kotaku "rocket to Jupiter" quote](https://kotaku.com/kojima-calls-guerrillas-decima-engine-a-rocket-that-wil-1789979728)
- In 2026, Kojima Productions' CTO told Automaton why the studio has stuck with Decima for nearly ten years. PC Gamer's headline paraphrases him: the 13-year-old engine lets the team "accomplish many things that would be difficult to achieve elsewhere." — [Automaton West](https://automaton-media.com/en/interviews/we-ask-kojima-productions-why-theyve-stuck-to-decima-for-nearly-ten-years-chief-technology-officer-on-what-makes-death-stranding/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/13-year-old-decima-engine-allows-death-stranding-team-to-accomplish-many-things-that-would-be-difficult-to-achieve-elsewhere/); [Push Square (Feb 2026)](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2026/02/heres-why-decima-engine-was-the-top-choice-for-death-stranding-and-kojima-productions). The article bodies wouldn't fetch, so I don't have the CTO's name or verbatim quotes.

**id Tech 7/8 (id Software)**
- Director of Engine Technology Billy Khan gave Digital Foundry a long interview on building id Tech 8 for Doom: The Dark Ages (2025). Topics: why the game requires hardware ray tracing, id's aim to avoid the shader-compilation / traversal stutter associated with other engines ("stutter struggle"), path tracing taking about six months to add, and work on a Nanite-like virtual geometry system. — [Digital Foundry interview, via ResetEra thread](https://www.resetera.com/threads/digital-foundry-inside-doom-the-dark-ages-creating-id-tech-8-interview-with-id-software.1192350/); [N4G](https://n4g.com/news/2667254/inside-doom-the-dark-ages-creating-id-tech-8-interview-with-id-software); [NVIDIA dev blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-id-software-used-neural-rendering-and-path-tracing-in-doom-the-dark-ages/)

**Luminous Engine (Square Enix), abandoned, HISTORICAL**
- Only two games shipped on it: Final Fantasy XV and Forspoken. Luminous Productions ran from 2018 until it was merged into Square Enix in May 2023. Director Hajime Tabata left at the end of 2018 and about half the staff followed him to JP Games. — [Wikipedia: Luminous Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminous_Productions); [Wikipedia: Luminous Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminous_Engine)
- Final Fantasy XVI ruled out Luminous early. The FFXV team was still busy with DLC, so it hadn't written an engine manual and couldn't offer support. FFXVI's Creative Business Unit III used its own engine instead. — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/final-fantasy-xvi-engine-luminous/)

**Ubisoft Anvil / Snowdrop**
- Ubisoft has consolidated mainly on two engines: Anvil (Assassin's Creed, systemic open worlds) and Snowdrop (Massive). Anvil was presented at SIGGRAPH 2025 and used for AC Shadows. — [Wikipedia: Ubisoft Anvil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubisoft_Anvil); [Wikipedia: Snowdrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdrop_(game_engine)); [GamesMarket on AC Shadows](https://www.gamesmarket.global/under-the-hood-ubisofts-anvil-engine-in-assassins-creed-shadows/)

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern in the testimony is genre fit. Frostbite was praised for Battlefield 6 and cursed for BioWare RPGs. RE Engine is praised for Resident Evil and criticized for Dragon's Dogma 2 and Wilds. An in-house engine encodes the assumptions of the team and genre it was built for.
- Organizational fit matters as much as technical fit. The Frostbite central team was understaffed. Luminous had no manual or support for another team. REDengine was "super centralised into one technology." Each case is a failure to scale an engine to several parallel teams, not a failure of rendering quality.
- Oblivion Remastered points to a hybrid model: keep the legacy simulation engine and put UE5 on top as the renderer.

- No primary sources gathered for Source/Source 2, Northlight (Remedy), Fox Engine (Konami), Glacier (IOI), 4A Engine, Rockstar RAGE, or Indiana Jones on id Tech (MachineGames). These need a follow-up search.

- Verbatim quotes from the Kojima Productions CTO (2026, Automaton) and the BF6 technical director (TweakTown) couldn't be retrieved.
- No verified 2024–2026 BioWare developer testimony on Frostbite during Veilguard.
- No verified detail on Ubisoft's 2025–2026 reorganization (e.g., "Vantage Studios") and what it means for its engines.

## Q2. What are the systemic pros and cons of in-house vs commercial engines, according to developer testimony?

### Takeaway
Studios that switch to Unreal cite the same reasons again and again: running several projects in parallel, hiring and onboarding, not having to build technology, access to Nanite/Lumen, and the staff cost of maintaining an engine. They rarely say the old engine was bad. Studios that stay in-house (id, Kojima/Guerrilla, DICE for BF6) cite performance, control, stutter avoidance and fitting the engine to their genre.

### Cited Findings
- **Parallel projects and sharing tech across teams.** This is CDPR's main stated reason for the switch, per Tremblay. — [Gamepressure/Eurogamer](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/cd-projekt-explained-its-decision-to-switch-from-redengine-to-ue5/z67609). Halo Studios also cites running multiple teams in parallel. — [Pure Xbox](https://www.purexbox.com/news/2024/10/halo-dev-explains-decision-behind-ditching-its-old-game-engine)
- **Hiring and onboarding.** Halo Studios' COO asked how long it takes to "ramp somebody up." — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2024/10/06/halo-studios-unreal-engine-interview/)
- **Engine upkeep eats staff.** Parts of Slipspace were about 25 years old, and staff were diverted to maintaining it. — [Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2024/10/06/halo-studios-unreal-engine-interview/); [Pure Xbox](https://www.purexbox.com/news/2024/10/halo-dev-explains-decision-behind-ditching-its-old-game-engine)
- **"We never wanted to make technology."** CDPR's co-CEO. — [FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/news/as-cyberpunk-2-continues-on-unreal-cd-projekt-ceo-says-redengine-was-not-really-the-issue-they-just-never-wanted-to-make-technology/)
- **Typical in-house pains:** poor documentation, hacked-together tools, slow iteration, basic features missing outside the original genre. — [Kotaku (Anthem)](https://kotaku.com/how-biowares-anthem-went-wrong-1833731964); [Kotaku (Andromeda)](https://kotaku.com/the-story-behind-mass-effect-andromedas-troubled-five-1795886428)
- **Knowledge walks out the door.** Luminous went unused after its team left or was busy, and no manual existed. — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/final-fantasy-xvi-engine-luminous/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminous_Productions)
- **Pro in-house: performance and control.** id Tech 8 aims to avoid UE-style stutter and is tuned to its game. — [DF interview via ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/digital-foundry-inside-doom-the-dark-ages-creating-id-tech-8-interview-with-id-software.1192350/). BF6 re-engineered Frostbite memory handling for Series S. — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/battlefield-6-runs-shockingly-well-on-xbox-series-s)
- **Pro in-house: a genre-fit shared engine can be a multiplier.** Kojima picked Decima for its open-world tools, and the two studios co-develop it. — [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/02/23/the-hideo-kojima-death-stranding-interview-strands-decima-and-guerrilla-games/); [Push Square 2026](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2026/02/heres-why-decima-engine-was-the-top-choice-for-death-stranding-and-kojima-productions)
- **Commercial engines aren't turnkey either.** CDPR judged UE5 "not ready" at the entry level and had to co-develop changes with Epic. — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/cd-projekt-explained-its-decision-to-switch-from-redengine-to-ue5/z67609)
- **Industry trend pieces** say custom engines add 18–24 months to development, that 30–40% of engine work goes to legacy compatibility, and quote an anonymous developer: "40% of our engineering budget just keeping the engine competitive with UE5." — [unrealsolutions.com](https://unrealsolutions.com/insights/game-engine-trends-2026.html); [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/game-studios-turn-to-unreal-engine-5-as-proprietary-tools-fall-from-favor). **Caution:** these are low-quality sources with unnamed quotes and unsourced statistics, and one is an Unreal services vendor. Don't present them as facts.

### Inferences
- The UE5 migrations (CDPR, Halo Studios) are framed mainly as organizational and scaling decisions: parallel projects, labor market, keeping up with features. They aren't framed as "our engine couldn't render."
- The studios that stay in-house successfully all have a long-lived dedicated engine team, a narrow genre focus, and first-party or platform backing (id/Microsoft, Guerrilla/Sony, DICE/EA).
- The trade-off works in both directions. UE5 brings hiring and feature benefits but inherits UE's known stutter and CPU issues, which is exactly what id cites as its reason to stay in-house.

### Gaps
- No GDC Vault "build vs buy" talk was located or verified in this pass.

- Whether Frostbite is still mandated EA-wide in 2026 (e.g., for the next Mass Effect) wasn't verified.
